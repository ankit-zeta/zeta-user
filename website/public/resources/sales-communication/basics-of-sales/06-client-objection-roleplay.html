Client Objection Roleplay Scenarios

Practice handling real objections with these ready-to-use scripts

💰 Scenario 1: Price Objection

"Your price is too high"
Prospect: "I like what you're offering, but honestly, your price is way too high. I can get similar services for half the cost."
You: "I appreciate you being upfront about that. Can I ask — when you say 'similar services,' are you comparing apples to apples? What specifically are you looking at?"
Prospect: "Well, there's another company offering the same thing for ₹20,000 less."
You: "Got it. Let me ask you this — if I could show you why our clients choose us despite the price difference, would that be worth a 5-minute conversation? Because there's a reason [Client Name] switched to us from a cheaper provider."

⏰ Scenario 2: Timing Objection

"Now isn't a good time"
Prospect: "I'm interested, but honestly, now isn't a good time. We're swamped with other projects."
You: "I completely understand. What would need to change for it to be a better time?"
Prospect: "Probably after Q2 ends. Things calm down then."
You: "Makes sense. What if we scheduled a quick 15-minute call right before Q2 ends? That way you're ready to go when things calm down. I can also send you a checklist to review in the meantime — no commitment, just so you're prepared."

🤝 Scenario 3: Trust Objection

"How do I know this will work?"
Prospect: "This sounds good, but I've been burned before. How do I know this will actually work for my business?"
You: "That's a completely fair concern. Can I share what happened with [Client Name]? They had the exact same worry."
Prospect: "Sure, go ahead."
You: "They came to us 6 months ago in a similar situation. They'd tried two other providers and got no results. We started with a small pilot project — just ₹10,000 to test. Within 3 months, they saw [specific result]. Now they're our biggest client. Would a similar pilot make sense for you?"

📋 Scenario 4: Authority Objection

"I need to discuss with my partner/boss"
Prospect: "This looks interesting, but I need to discuss this with my business partner before making any decisions."
You: "Absolutely, that makes sense. What do you think they'll be most concerned about?"
Prospect: "Probably the cost and the timeline."
You: "Got it. Would it be helpful if I put together a one-page summary addressing those two points? That way you have something concrete to show them. And if they have questions, I'm happy to hop on a quick call with both of you."

💡 Practice Tips

• Record yourself handling objections — listen back for filler words
• Practice with a friend who plays the prospect
• Start with the most common objections first
• Keep responses under 30 seconds — don't ramble
• Always have 2-3 backup responses ready
