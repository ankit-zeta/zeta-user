Growth Analytics Glossary

Essential terminology: CAC, LTV, churn, conversion rate, funnel, A/B test, cohort, attribution, NPS, and more

Core Growth Metrics

CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)
Total marketing + sales cost ÷ New customers acquired. Formula: Spend ÷ Customers. Must be less than LTV for sustainable growth. Benchmark: < 33% of LTV.
LTV (Lifetime Value)
Total revenue a customer generates over their relationship. Formula: Avg Revenue × Avg Customer Lifespan. Higher LTV = more you can spend to acquire customers.
LTV:CAC Ratio
Relationship between lifetime value and acquisition cost. Target: 3:1 or higher. Below 1:1 = losing money. 1:1-3:1 = unsustainable. 3:1+ = healthy growth.
Churn Rate
Percentage of customers who stop paying/using over a period. Formula: Lost Customers ÷ Starting Customers × 100. Monthly SaaS benchmark: 3-7%. Lower is better.
MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue)
Predictable monthly revenue from subscriptions. Formula: Sum of all monthly subscription values. SaaS North Star metric. Track MRR growth rate monthly.
ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue)
MRR × 12. Used for annual contracts and SaaS valuation. Investors value SaaS at 5-15x ARR depending on growth rate and retention.

Funnel & Conversion Terms

Conversion Rate
Percentage of visitors who complete a desired action. Formula: Conversions ÷ Total Visitors × 100. Landing page benchmark: 2-5%. Higher = more revenue per visitor.
Funnel
The path users take from first awareness to purchase (and beyond). Stages: Awareness → Interest → Consideration → Conversion → Retention. Drop-off at each stage = optimization opportunity.
Drop-off Rate
Percentage of users who leave at a specific funnel stage. Formula: (Users at Stage N - Users at Stage N+1) ÷ Users at Stage N × 100. High drop-off = broken stage.
Activation Rate
Percentage of users who complete the key action that predicts retention. SaaS: first project created. E-commerce: first purchase. Mobile app: 3 sessions in 7 days.
Bounce Rate
Percentage of visitors who leave after viewing only one page. Formula: Single-page sessions ÷ Total sessions × 100. Below 40% = good. Above 70% = problem (unless blog).
Time to Value (TTV)
How long it takes a new user to experience your product's core value. Shorter TTV = better activation = lower churn. Optimize onboarding to reduce TTV.

Testing & Experimentation

A/B Test (Split Test)
Comparing two versions (A and B) to see which performs better. One variable changed, all others identical. Requires: statistical significance (95%+), adequate sample size, sufficient duration.
Statistical Significance
The probability that test results aren't due to random chance. Target: 95%+ confidence. Below 95%: results may be noise. Use a calculator — don't guess.
Sample Size
Number of observations needed for reliable results. Minimum: 1,000 visitors per variation for conversion tests. Smaller samples = unreliable results. Calculate before starting test.
Minimum Viable Experiment
The simplest version of a test that provides actionable data. Don't over-engineer tests. Landing page: change headline. Email: change subject line. Fast tests, fast learning.
Bayesian vs Frequentist
Frequentist: p-value approach, binary result (significant or not). Bayesian: probability-based, gives "X% chance B beats A." Bayesian is more intuitive for business decisions.
Guardrail Metrics
Secondary metrics you monitor during tests to ensure you're not hurting other goals. Example: Testing a shorter form (primary: CVR) but monitoring lead quality (guardrail).

Retention & Cohort Terms

Cohort
A group of users who share a characteristic within a defined time period. Example: "Users who signed up in January 2026." Cohorts reveal trends hidden in aggregate data.
NPS (Net Promoter Score)
Customer loyalty metric: "How likely are you to recommend us?" Score 0-10. Promoters (9-10) minus Detractors (0-6). Target: 50+. Correlates strongly with growth.
Retention Curve
Graph showing % of users returning over time. Steepest drop-off is usually Day 1-7. Curve flattening = healthy retention. If curve keeps declining = product problem.
North Star Metric
The single metric that best captures the core value your product delivers to customers. Examples: Airbnb = nights booked, Slack = messages sent, Netflix = hours watched.
Leading vs Lagging Indicators
Leading: Predict future outcomes (signups, activation rate). Lagging: Confirm past results (revenue, churn). Track leading metrics to act before problems show in revenue.
Growth Loop
Self-reinforcing cycle where output becomes input. Example: User invites friend → Friend signs up → Friend invites another. Viral loops = sustainable, compounding growth.

Pro Tip

You don't need to memorize every term. Start with CAC, LTV, and conversion rate. Add churn if you're subscription-based. Everything else you'll learn as you encounter it. The goal is action, not vocabulary.
